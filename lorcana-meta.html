What Wins at Disney Lorcana's Biggest Tournaments?

A data project analyzing top-placing decks from 19 Disney Lorcana Challenges, 2024–2026.

Role

Data collection, modeling,
analysis, visualization

Tools

Python, Tableau, Google Sheets / Excel,
Jira, Claude

Data

73 decklists · 19 events
3,500+ card printings

Data: top-cut decklists from Disney Lorcana Challenges with 500+ players. Play rate = share of collected decklists that include the card. Franchise share = share of all card copies played that come from each Disney franchise.

Key findings

  • Ursula – Deceiver appeared in 40% of top-placing decklists, more than any other card.
  • Steel cards held 3 of the 5 most-played spots, including Strength of a Raging Fire (37%).
  • The most-played cards span four of the six inks, so no single ink dominated the meta.
  • The Little Mermaid is the most-played franchise at 8% of all card copies, about half of them Ursula. The Sword in the Stone (7%) and Sleeping Beauty (6%) follow.

Exploring the data in a spreadsheet

The dashboard shows which cards win. Afterward, I used pivot tables in Google Sheets to ask a follow-up question: has the kind of card that wins changed over time? It has. In 2025, top decks leaned almost entirely on characters, while songs and items nearly vanished. Both returned in 2026.

How I built it

  • Collected the data. Tournament results aren't published in one place, so I compiled 73 top-cut decklists from official recaps and community coverage and recorded the source for every one.
  • Pulled every card. A Python script downloads all 3,500+ card printings (2,741 unique cards) from the Lorcast card API.
  • Matched and cleaned. A second Python script matches each decklist card to its details (ink, type, cost and keywords), flags any card it can't match, and outputs clean tables for Tableau.
  • Mapped every card to a franchise. Matched each card to its Disney film using card data, flavor text and a franchise lookup table, and fixed dual-ink cards so both colors show.
  • Visualized. I built the dashboard in Tableau and published it to Tableau Public.
  • Dug deeper with pivot tables. After the dashboard, I used Google Sheets to compare card types by year and the most-played cards in Top 8 decks.
  • Managed the work. I tracked the project as user stories with acceptance criteria in Jira.
  • Worked with AI. I used Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, throughout the project: planning the data model, writing and debugging the Python scripts, designing the dashboards and building this page.
