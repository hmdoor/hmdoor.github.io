About Me

Bridging people and technology

I'm a Senior Application Support Engineer with 7+ years in software support, DevOps and cloud operations. I'm at my best connecting development and operations teams, turning code into stable, scalable cloud environments and keeping systems reliable. I helped build a formal training program for incoming engineers, ran hands-on training for support engineers and the documentation I wrote is still in use today. Earlier in my career, I built custom CMS features, then led client training sessions and wrote success manuals so clients could get the most out of them. I've also enjoyed working closely with UX and getting hands-on Figma experience. I'm now looking for my next role in project management, business analysis, application support, or learning and development and training.

My love of technology started with my first computer in 3rd grade and a heavily customized MySpace page. That curiosity led me to Tech Elevator, and I haven't stopped building since. These days that means Python, Tableau and Jira, working alongside AI tools like Claude.

Before tech, I earned a BA in Music and Business from Otterbein University. After graduating, I spent a fellowship year with the Cleveland Leadership Center learning to work with people across every sector, age and background. I carried that into fundraising at Beck Center for the Arts, where I built relationships with donors and planned events from first idea to final thank-you note. That's the skill I lean on most: understanding what people need, then building something that works for them.

Off the clock, you'll usually find me across the table from someone at a Disney Lorcana tournament. Playing competitively keeps my brain sharp. Every match means reading the board, planning several turns ahead, adapting when things go sideways and reading the person across from me. Competitive play is also great practice for my day job: making decisions under pressure, spotting patterns, managing risk and staying calm (and kind) when the game doesn't go my way. Win or lose, I walk away with a handshake and a lesson for the next round. When I'm not shuffling cards, you can find me on a bike, working through a growing stack of books, or hosting Craft-ernoon: a monthly afternoon of crafting, snacks and good company.

My Projects

Things I've built

Lorcana Tournament Manager:

A web app I'm building to run my own Disney Lorcana tournaments: player registration, Swiss pairings that avoid rematches, best-of-3 results, standings with standard tiebreakers, a top cut bracket and a projector round timer. Built in Python with a JSON API and browser front end, it saves every change to a SQLite database and includes undo, 59 automated tests and a documented database design. I tracked the work in Jira as user stories with acceptance criteria, and I'm gathering requirements from local store organizers and players to shape it.

See screenshots and demo →

Lorcana Challenge Meta Dashboards:

A Tableau dashboard series analyzing every Disney Lorcana Challenge with 500+ players since May 2024. Python scripts pull every card from the Lorcast API, match it against published top-cut decklists, and build a clean dataset with a data dictionary and quality checks, showing which inks, cards and Disney franchises win at the highest level. I built it with help from Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant.

See the dashboard and findings →

National Park Weather Service:

For this capstone project, my partner and I created a multi-page website (using Spring Web MVC) where users are able to select from a list of national parks to view the park details and weather in celsius or fahrenheit. Users are also able to add surveys for the parks. Once the survey is submitted, it is stored in a database (Postgres) and the user is redirected to a page that displays all survey results, pulled in from the database.

Java-Jam: Princess Edition

This is a side project I've modified to really understand the principles of object-oriented programming. In this command line game, users are able to choose from a list of 10 different Disney princesses, assign them a weapon and watch as they fight to the death! This project was created in Eclipse to run as a Java application.
